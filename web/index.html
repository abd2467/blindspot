Why radar

Smoke hides the fire. Radar doesn't.

During Indonesia's 2026 fire season, thick smoke and cloud hid Kalimantan from the optical satellites that build the fire record. NISAR's L-band radar looks straight through.

01

The record goes quiet when it matters most

Indonesia tracks fires with the MODIS and VIIRS instruments, which detect heat. NASA notes that the recorded fire count can fall when fires are most intense, because thick smoke and cloud block the view. Peat also smoulders underground, often too cool to register.

02

A 24 cm wave passes through smoke

NISAR sends its own microwave pulse at L-band, a wavelength of about 24 cm, far longer than smoke particles or cloud droplets, so it passes through them. It works day and night and returns to the same place every 12 days.

03

What a burn looks like to radar

The cross-polarised HV signal comes mostly from scattering inside the forest canopy. When the canopy burns or is cleared, HV drops and stays down. In peatland, L-band separated burned from unburned land with 93 to 96% overall accuracy in a recent Indonesian study.

From radar to an audit

BlindSpot checks every patch of radar-detected change against the hotspot record. Where none was recorded, the patch becomes a place to look: a candidate disturbance, not proof of fire.

Method

How BlindSpot works, and how sure it is.

A statistical test on radar change, a cross-check against the fire record, and an honest range around every number.

01

Five steps

  1. 01
    Prepare
    Stream only our 50 km study window from each NISAR GCOV image, HH and HV, and average it onto one 60 m grid.
  2. 02
    Normalise
    Divide each date by its scene-wide ratio to the June and July reference, so rain, soil moisture and processing changes cancel.
  3. 03
    Detect
    A sequential omnibus test (Conradsen 2016; Canty 2020) flags change at a 1% false-alarm rate. HV must stay at least 1 dB down for two dates; patches of 1 ha or more are kept.
  4. 04
    Cross-check
    A patch counts as recorded if a VIIRS or MODIS hotspot lies within 1 km of it between the previous radar date and the change date.
  5. 05
    Validate
    Synthetic burns test what we can detect; a Monte Carlo run gives the range; a labelled sample gives the error-adjusted area with a 95% interval (Olofsson 2014).
